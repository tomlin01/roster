# Lesson Brief

## Chunk
- Topic: Probability axioms, first exposure
- Owner: Teacher
- Status: promoted
- Intent: give the producer a stable teaching contract for the first chunk
- Target audience: first-time learner with basic event intuition but weak symbolic fluency
- Estimated duration: 90 seconds

## Learning Goal
- By the end of this chunk, the learner should be able to say what the three probability axioms are trying to guarantee:
  - probabilities do not go below zero,
  - the whole sample space has probability one,
  - disjoint events add.

## Must-Be-Correct Points
- Probability is never negative: $P(A) \ge 0$.
- The full sample space has probability one: $P(\Omega) = 1$.
- For disjoint events, additivity holds: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$.

## Can-Be-Simplified Points
- The chunk does not need to prove the axioms.
- The chunk can describe `disjoint` with plain language before using the formal term repeatedly.

## Pre-Required Intuition
- The learner has seen simple events such as drawing a card or flipping a coin.
- The learner knows that probability describes how likely an event is.

## Likely Confusions
- Why the sample space must equal one instead of “100 percent” being treated as a separate concept.
- Why additivity only applies to disjoint events.
- Losing track of notation when the union symbol appears too early.

## Teaching Order
1. Start with the idea that probabilities cannot be negative.
2. State that the whole sample space is the “all outcomes” case and has probability one.
3. Introduce disjoint events in plain language.
4. Show additivity only after disjointness is made concrete.

## Open Questions
- none after this teacher pass; use coin toss outcomes for the first beginner-facing example
